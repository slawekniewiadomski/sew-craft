# 3D preview of a sewn assembly

Status: **implemented** — direction agreed in
`.ai/specs/briefs/2026-11-07-3d-preview.md`; renderer and solver placement settled
in ADR-0011. Built on `feat/3d-preview`, stacked on the seam branch; the ordered
steps and commits are in `.ai/runs/2026-11-07-3d-preview.md`.

Builds on: `.ai/specs/2026-11-07-seams-and-sewability.md` (seam records, finished
edges, `resolveSeamRun`) and the assembly placement math in
`docs/architecture/geometry-core.md`.
No schema change. No command. The preview is derived, read-only view state.

## 📝 TLDR

A **Preview** action on the assembly tab opens a full-screen 3D view with two
modes:

- **Exploded** (default) — every placed piece is a flat cut part on its own layer,
  with its stitch-loop seam sides highlighted and dashed links between the sides
  that sew together. No folding and no solver: this is the honest assembly
  diagram, and it works for a gusset strip too.
- **Sewn** — panels are triangulated, seam sides are welded, and a deterministic
  height-field inflation raises each panel's interior on a rounded profile so a
  pouch reads as a rounded pouch and a garment as a stuffed silhouette. When a
  strip joins two faces of the same piece, a **closed gusset box** is built
  instead: two flat caps and a vertical wall, at the strip's true width.

Orbit is shared; the **Inflate** slider belongs to the sewn mode. The result is
approximate by design; it never blocks editing and is never stored.

## 🎯 Goals

- Show the sewn assembly as a recognisable 3D dummy within a second of opening.
- Use the seam record as the only input: layer order decides which panel inflates
  away from which; `reversed` decides correspondence; finished edges stay open.
- Show which edges sew to which before any folding guess: the exploded view is
  derived from the same seam record, so a gusset strip and its nine seams are
  legible even where the sewn shell is still approximate.
- Stay inside the locked SVG stack (ADR-0011): orthographic projection, flat
  shading, painter's algorithm, no WebGL dependency.
- Keep the simulation pure and tested in `@sewcraft/geometry`.

## 🚫 Non-goals

- No gravity, collision, self-collision, friction or time integration.
- No body/mannequin collider, no fit testing, no cloth parameters (bending
  stiffness is a constant).
- No textures, smooth normals, shadows, transparency or anti-aliased mesh edges.
- No 3D export, no saved camera or pose, no animation.
- No mid-edge seam spans; a seam still addresses whole loop edges.
- No change to the document, command spec or schema.

## 🧠 Model

### Panels

Each assembly instance contributes one panel: its **stitch loop** in assembly
space (`instanceLoops(...).stitch`). Corner-round bridges are part of the loop.
The loop is triangulated in 2D by ear clipping, then uniformly subdivided by
midpoint bisection until every triangle edge is at most `PREVIEW_MAX_EDGE`
(18 mm). The subdivision adds interior vertices; boundary vertices stay intact.

A panel whose loop does not resolve is skipped and reported in `skipped`
(instance ids). The preview still renders the remaining panels.

### Exploded view

`explodedAssembly(document, assembly)` is the second, solver-free model. Every
instance contributes one flat part: its **cut loop** (the fabric shape,
allowances included) at `z = index * EXPLODE_GAP` (`EXPLODE_GAP = 60` mm), with
the label anchored at the part's centroid. Instances whose piece or loop does not
resolve are listed in `skipped`. The part label is the piece name, with an
occurrence suffix when the same piece is placed twice (`Panel 1`, `Panel 2`), and
`Piece N` when the piece has no name.

For every seam, `resolveSeamRun` gives each resolvable side's stitch polyline at
its part's layer. A dashed **link** joins the arc-length midpoint of every other
side to the midpoint of side 0, and each seam gets a rotating palette index
(`SEAM_COLOR_COUNT = 12`). A seam with fewer than two resolvable sides is
omitted. There is no folding, no welding and no triangle budget: the view is a
deterministic diagram of the parts and their seams.

### Gusset box

A strip whose seams reach exactly two face instances (at least two seams each),
where both faces place the same piece and the strip is a different piece, is a
gusset. `drapeAssembly` detects it after alignment and returns a closed box
instead of the height field:

- The **front face's aligned stitch loop** is the common perimeter. Two flat caps
  sit at `z = ±inflate × width / 2`; the caps use the same loop, so a congruent
  back face is implied even when it is mirrored or oddly placed.
- The **wall** is one vertical quad per front-loop segment, using the strip
  piece's colour. Its winding follows the loop's signed area, so a concave or
  clockwise face still points outward.
- The strip **width** is its area over its principal extent (the major axis from
  the loop covariance), so a strip split into four 60 mm perimeter edges still
  measures 240 × 40 and yields a 40 mm deep box.
- Any other shape — a face with fewer than two seams to the strip, unequal face
  pieces, a strip too short or wide, a non-simplex strip — falls back to the
  height field. The box mesh is closed (`openBoundaries: []`) and reports
  `simplified: false`.

This is a deliberately symmetric box, not a fitted 3D assembly: it reads the
fabric strip as the wall and the two faces as the end caps, which is the shape a
gusseted pouch is meant to be.

### Seam correspondence

For each seam side:

1. `resolveSeamRun` gives the assembly-space side polyline, bridges included.
2. Sample it at `N = max(2, ceil(length / PREVIEW_SAMPLE_SPACING))` intervals,
   with `PREVIEW_SAMPLE_SPACING = 8` mm. Samples are at `i / N` of the side length,
   from the side's start (honouring `reversed`).
3. The sample points are inserted into the panel's stitch loop as boundary
   vertices: the side is a contiguous loop slice, so each sample's arc position
   maps to a loop position, and the loop is rebuilt with the samples in place.
4. The k-th sample of every side of one seam shares a **weld group**. Welding
   merges those boundary vertices into one, which closes the shell and keeps the
   seam edges from opening under inflation.

A side that cannot be mapped onto its panel loop (a hand-edited reference) is
skipped; the seam contributes no weld. This never throws.

### Layer order and panel direction

A seam's `sides` order is layer order. Side 0 gets direction `+1`, every other
side `-1`; a panel accumulates the directions of the seams it belongs to and uses
the sign of the sum (ties stay `+1`). Two layers of a pouch therefore inflate in
opposite directions, while a dart sewn to itself stays neutral.

### Inflation (height field)

The shell is not relaxed. Each panel keeps its flat layout and is raised on a
**height field** over that layout:

- **Distance.** Every vertex gets its shortest distance to the panel's stitch
  boundary. The seam, finished edges and open boundaries are distance zero, so
  the whole boundary stays on the seam plane `z = 0`.
- **Profile.** The height is `sign × inflate × maxDistance × PREVIEW_THICKNESS ×
  smoothstep(min(distance / radius, 1))`, with `PREVIEW_THICKNESS = 1.0`,
  `radius = maxDistance × PREVIEW_EDGE` and `PREVIEW_EDGE = 0.3`. The smoothstep
  gives a flat-ish centre and a rounded edge; the whole panel is a smooth dome.
- **Sign.** A panel's direction comes from the seam layer order (side 0 `+1`,
  every other side `-1`); the front and back panels therefore rise on opposite
  sides of the seam plane and meet at the rim.
- **Mesh.** The seam samples become boundary vertices, a **centroid fan** gives
  the panel a symmetric apex and rings, and conforming subdivision refines it to
  `PREVIEW_MAX_EDGE`. A concave panel whose centre fan would leave the loop falls
  back to the ear-clipped mesh.
- **Outward faces.** Triangles of a `-1` panel are wound the other way, so every
  normal points away from the shell; the renderer culls the faces pointing away
  from the camera and shades the visible outward surface.

No distances, bending, gravity, time step or iteration count are involved. The
same document, seam record and inflate value always produce the same mesh, and
the slider only changes the height.

### Open boundaries

After welding, boundary edges (used by one triangle) form one or more loops.
They stay open: finished edges are the object's openings, and a garment preview
shows them. No caps are added; inflation comes from panel direction, not a global
volume constraint.

### Cost guard

`PREVIEW_TRIANGLE_LIMIT = 6000`. If the built mesh exceeds it, the preview keeps
the largest connected shell (triangle adjacency through shared edges) and sets
`simplified: true`. The dialog says so; the geometry never loops forever trying to
fit the budget.

## 📝 API Contracts

```ts
export const PREVIEW_SAMPLE_SPACING = 8;
export const PREVIEW_MAX_EDGE = 18;
export const PREVIEW_TRIANGLE_LIMIT = 6000;

export interface Vec3 {
  x: number;
  y: number;
  z: number;
}

export interface PreviewTriangle {
  a: number;
  b: number;
  c: number;
  /** Index into `panels`; the panel's colour and shading group. */
  panel: number;
}

export interface PreviewPanel {
  instance: EntityId;
  piece: EntityId;
  color: string | null;
}

export interface PreviewMesh {
  /** Final 3D positions, in assembly millimetres. */
  positions: Vec3[];
  triangles: PreviewTriangle[];
  panels: PreviewPanel[];
  /** Boundary loops that stayed open, as vertex-index cycles. */
  openBoundaries: number[][];
  /** True when the triangle budget forced a simplification. */
  simplified: boolean;
  /** Instances whose loop did not resolve. */
  skipped: EntityId[];
}

export interface DrapeOptions {
  /** 0–1; the slider. Defaults to 0.6. */
  inflate?: number;
}

export function drapeAssembly(
  document: PatternDocument,
  assembly: Assembly,
  options?: DrapeOptions,
): PreviewMesh | null;
```

`drapeAssembly` returns `null` only when the assembly has no resolvable panel. It
is pure, total and deterministic; an empty or broken assembly yields `null` or a
mesh with `skipped`, never a throw.

The exploded model is a second pure function in `packages/geometry/src/exploded.ts`:

```ts
export interface ExplodedPanel {
  instance: EntityId;
  piece: EntityId;
  label: string;
  color: string | null;
  /** The cut-loop boundary, in 3D, without a repeated closing point. */
  boundary: Vec3[];
  /** The part's label anchor. */
  center: Vec3;
}

export interface ExplodedSeam {
  id: EntityId;
  sides: { instance: EntityId; points: Vec3[] }[];
  /** Dashes from every other side's midpoint to side 0's midpoint. */
  links: Vec3[][];
  colorIndex: number;
}

export interface ExplodedView {
  panels: ExplodedPanel[];
  seams: ExplodedSeam[];
  skipped: EntityId[];
}

export function explodedAssembly(
  document: PatternDocument,
  assembly: Assembly,
): ExplodedView | null;
```

The frontend's pure projection lives in `frontend/src/lib/preview-mesh.ts`:

```ts
export interface PreviewPolygon {
  /** SVG `points` for one projected triangle. */
  points: string;
  /** `#rrggbb` fill after flat shading. */
  fill: string;
  /** Painter's-algorithm depth; larger is nearer. */
  depth: number;
  panel: number;
}

export function projectMesh(
  mesh: PreviewMesh,
  view: { yaw: number; pitch: number },
  options?: { wireframe?: boolean },
): { polygons: PreviewPolygon[]; bounds: { width: number; height: number } };
```

The projection is orthographic: rotate by yaw around Z, then pitch around X,
drop Z, sort polygons by the centroid's view depth ascending, and shade each face
with `lambert = 0.25 + 0.75 * |normal · light|` against a fixed light direction.
The fill is the panel colour darkened/lightened by `lambert`; an absent colour
uses the neutral fallback. Wireframe keeps the outline and uses a transparent
fill.

`projectExploded(view, camera)` reuses the same rotation. It returns one SVG
`points` string per part boundary (painted back to front by view depth), the
projected seam sides and dashed links, the label anchors, and the shared bounds.
Parts use the piece colour at 32 % opacity; seam sides and links use the rotating
palette (`#4f7cac`, `#5b9e6f`, `#c08457`, `#9b6b9e`, `#b05c6b`, `#5a9aa8`, `#a3934a`, `#7d6fb0`, `#b07a4a`, `#4f9a86`, `#a05c8a`, `#6b7fb0`).

## 📝 UI/UX

- **Preview** is an assembly-ribbon action (new `RibbonAction` `preview`),
  disabled while read-only? No: the preview is read-only, so it stays enabled
  even in a read-only document, like Fit and Download.
- It opens a full-screen `ModalDialog`-style panel:
  - header: “Preview · <assembly name>”, an **Exploded | Sewn** toggle, and a close
    button. **Exploded** is the default;
  - a large SVG viewport with a neutral background;
  - exploded hint: “Drag to orbit · exploded layers · hover a seam to isolate
    it”; hovering a seam's side or link dims every other seam to 12 % and
    thickens the focused one;
  - **part colours:** a stored piece colour wins; without one, each panel takes a
    deterministic colour from an eight-colour soft palette by panel order. The
    two faces of a pouch and a gusset strip are therefore different colours even
    when the pieces share the default colour;
  - sewn hint: “Drag to orbit · approximate preview”;
  - sewn controls: **Inflate** range 0–100 (default 100), **Wireframe** toggle;
    **Reset view** is always available;
  - a footer note (sewn only) when `simplified` (“Large pattern: the largest shell
    is shown”) or when the sewability report has errors (“Not sewable: the
    preview is approximate”). Warnings alone show nothing.
  - an empty state when the active mode resolves nothing: “Nothing to preview.”
- Orbit: pointer drag on the SVG changes yaw (horizontal) and pitch (vertical,
  clamped to ±80°). Double-click or **Reset view** returns to yaw −35°, pitch
  −25°.
- The preview is ephemeral: closing it discards the camera and inflate value is
  reset to the default on the next open (v1 simplification).
- Keyboard: Escape closes; the controls are ordinary form elements. The viewport
  is not a focusable 3D scene.

## 📝 Edge Cases & Failure Scenarios

- **Empty assembly**: the dialog shows “Nothing to preview.” in both modes.
- **Gusset with a missing face or a different face piece**: the box is not
  recognised and the sewn preview falls back to the height field.
- **Gusset with a short, wide or asymmetric strip**: falls back rather than
  guessing a box; the exploded view still shows the parts and seams.
- **Exploded with a broken part or seam**: the part is skipped (or the seam has no
  link) and the remaining parts still render; the sewn mesh is unaffected.
- **Exploded with no seams**: the flat parts render without links.
- **A broken panel loop**: skipped; other panels still render.
- **A broken seam reference**: no weld for it; the panels still render apart.
- **A one-sided seam** (hand-edited): no weld; panels render apart.
- **A seam with a huge length mismatch**: the weld still uses normalized arc
  length; the shorter side compresses, the preview is approximate.
- **A three-layer seam**: side 0 gets +1, sides 1 and 2 get −1; the middle layer
  inflates with the outer layer. The report still governs sewability.
- **A dart on one instance**: the two sides are on the same panel; the weld closes
  the dart, and the panel direction stays neutral.
- **Two shells in one assembly**: both render, fitted together; the triangle
  budget keeps the largest if needed.
- **A very large pattern**: the triangle budget keeps the largest shell and says
  so; no freeze.
- **Read-only document**: Preview stays available; nothing mutates.
- **Preview open while the document changes** (undo, autosave): the mesh is
  recomputed from the current document; the camera and inflate stay.
- **Closing the tab/dialog**: no state is written.

## 📋 Implementation Plan

1. **Geometry primitives.** Add `triangulate` (ear clipping) and `subdivide`
   (conforming midpoint bisection) to `packages/geometry/src/drape.ts` with tests
   for a square, a concave polygon, a collinear vertex and the edge-length cap.
2. **Panel assembly.** Build the stitch-loop panel, insert seam samples, map the
   side arc positions, and expose `buildPreviewPanels` with tests for a straight
   seam, a corner-round bridge, `reversed`, a mirrored instance and a broken loop.
3. **Welding and solver.** Union-find the weld groups, drop degenerate triangles,
   run the height-field inflate, compute open boundaries, apply
   the triangle budget, and test edge-length preservation, thickness growth,
   determinism, open vs closed shells and the skip paths.
4. **Projection.** Add `preview-mesh.ts` with orthographic projection, painter
   sorting, Lambert shading and bounds; test a single triangle, orbit clamping,
   wireframe and colour fallback.
5. **Dialog and ribbon.** Add `PreviewDialog.vue`, the `preview` ribbon action and
   icon, and the `EditorView` wiring; test open/close, the empty state, the slider
   and wireframe toggles, orbit drag and the approximate/simplified notes.
6. **Exploded view.** Add `explodedAssembly` (flat cut parts on layers, seam
   sides, midpoint links, labels) and `projectExploded`; add the Exploded | Sewn
   toggle with Exploded as the default. Test part layering, labels, skipped
   parts, one-sided seams, palette cycling and the projection; add a gusset
   exploded screenshot.
7. **Gusset box.** Detect a strip joining two same-piece faces, build two flat
   caps and a vertical wall at the strip's true width, orient every triangle
   outward and fall back for every ambiguous shape. Test the box, Inflate, a
   clockwise face, and each fallback.
8. **Docs, evidence and delivery.** Document the preview contract in
   `geometry-core.md` and the data model (no schema change), add integration
   screenshots of the exploded and sewn pouch previews, run the full gate, and
   open a stacked PR against `feat/seams-and-sewability`.
